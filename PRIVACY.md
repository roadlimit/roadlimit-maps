# RoadLimit privacy policy

Last updated: 31 October 2026

RoadLimit shows the speed limit for the road you're driving on in the UK. It has no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking. This policy explains the little it does with your data.

## Your location

RoadLimit uses your phone's location only while the app is open, to work out which road you're on and its speed limit. If you turn on Alerts with the screen off (Settings → Alerts → Alert settings), it carries on while you're driving with the screen off or another app in front, with a notification showing, and stops when the drive ends or you close RoadLimit. It never uses your location otherwise in the background, and it does not store or send your position anywhere.

To show limits, RoadLimit needs map data for the area around you. It gets it in one of two ways:

- **Map packs.** A whole region's map data, downloaded when you choose to from GitHub (github.com, run by GitHub, Inc.). The request says which region you chose, not where you are.
- **Map areas while driving.** Where there's no map pack, RoadLimit asks a public OpenStreetMap server for the roads in an area about 2 km across around you: overpass-api.de (FOSSGIS e.V., Germany), overpass.private.coffee or overpass.kumi.systems. The request contains that area, not your exact position. Like any website, these servers see your phone's internet address, and may keep it in their logs under their own policies.

Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, under the Open Database Licence.

## What stays on your phone

Your settings (licence plates, vehicle, sounds), the map data RoadLimit has downloaded, and RoadLimit's own log of what it did. The log can include the roads and places you drove that day. Old log entries are replaced automatically.

If you turn on **Record drives** on the Testing page, RoadLimit also saves your drives (positions and what it decided) on the phone for 30 days.

RoadLimit keeps a short list of where the speed limit it showed changed on your drives (the place, the road and the limit), on the phone for 7 days, so you can check them in **Settings → Check recent drives**. If you say a sign showed something different, RoadLimit uses your limit for that road on your phone only. It keeps each of these corrections, with the road's name, the place and the direction you were going (so it can find the road again when the map changes), until the map agrees or you remove it in Your corrections.

The **Open in OpenStreetMap** button in Check recent drives opens that place on openstreetmap.org in your browser, only when you tap it. OpenStreetMap's own privacy policy then applies.

None of this leaves your phone unless you choose to share it.

## When you share something

**Send feedback** (Settings → About) and **Share drives** (Testing page) make a file and open your email app with it attached, addressed to us at lekkyapp@gmail.com (or your phone's share menu if there's no email app). Nothing is sent until you send it, and you can change where it goes. Feedback contains only your note, RoadLimit's version, your phone's Android version and your settings. Share drives (for testers) contains your recorded drives, RoadLimit's log, the map areas used, and Check recent drives' list, your answers and your corrections (places, roads, limits and directions). We use what you send only to fix and improve RoadLimit, and delete it when it's no longer needed.

## Speech and sounds

Spoken limits use your phone's own text-to-speech. RoadLimit sends it only the words to say.

To stay quiet during phone calls, RoadLimit checks whether the phone is ringing or on a call (an ordinary or internet call). It never learns who is calling or anything about the call, and this check never leaves your phone.

If you turn on "Say 'wrong limit' to mark it" (Testing page), RoadLimit uses the microphone for 10 seconds after each spoken limit, listening only for the words "wrong limit". Speech is recognised on your phone by its own speech recognition; RoadLimit doesn't record or keep any audio, and nothing you say is sent anywhere. When it hears "wrong limit", it saves where the limit was spoken, the road and the limit shown, with your drives on the phone, so you can send it with Share drives. The microphone is never used otherwise, and the setting is off unless you turn it on.

## Children

RoadLimit is meant for drivers and isn't directed at children.

## Deleting your data

Uninstalling RoadLimit deletes everything it stored. You can also delete map packs and map areas saved while driving in Settings → Map data, recorded drives on the Testing page, and your corrections in Check recent drives.
